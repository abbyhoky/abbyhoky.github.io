## The interaction matters

Discussions about AI tutors often begin with capability: Can the system answer a question? Can it explain a concept? Can it generate practice exercises?

Those questions matter, but they do not fully describe the learning experience. Learners also interpret tone, reliability, responsiveness and the amount of control they retain during an interaction.

## From answers to learning relationships

An AI tutor can be treated as an answer engine, a practice partner, a source of feedback or a tool for testing ideas. These roles are not interchangeable. Each one creates different expectations about what the learner should contribute and what the system should provide.

Research into students’ use of AI should therefore attend to the practices around the tool: when learners turn to it, how they evaluate its suggestions and when they decide to seek human support.

## A question worth keeping

Rather than asking whether AI will replace a tutor, we might ask how learners distribute trust, effort and responsibility across human and AI support. That shift keeps the learner’s experience at the centre of the discussion.
