## Immersion is not the same as inclusion

Virtual reality can create vivid and memorable training experiences, but the presence of an immersive environment does not automatically make an experience accessible or culturally appropriate.

Language, familiarity with the technology, prior experience and the way a scenario is framed can all shape how participants respond.

## Start with participant perspectives

Interviews and focus groups can help uncover barriers that may not be visible during development. Participants can explain which instructions are clear, what feels unfamiliar and which parts of a scenario connect with their everyday experience.

## Design as an iterative process

Inclusive design is not a final checklist. It requires revisiting assumptions, testing prototypes and allowing participant feedback to influence the experience. The goal is not simply to introduce a new medium, but to make training more understandable, relevant and usable.
