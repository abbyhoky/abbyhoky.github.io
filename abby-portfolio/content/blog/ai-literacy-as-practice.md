## Beyond knowing the vocabulary

AI literacy is sometimes presented as a list of concepts: models, prompts, datasets, bias and hallucinations. These concepts are useful, but literacy also involves knowing what to do when a system produces an uncertain or persuasive answer.

## Learning through situated decisions

A learner may need to decide whether an answer is trustworthy, whether a task is appropriate to delegate, or how to acknowledge AI assistance. These are situated decisions shaped by context, purpose and consequences.

Educational activities can make these decisions visible. Students can compare outputs, explain why they trust one source over another and reflect on how their prompts influence the result.

## Make room for reflection

A strong AI literacy activity should not end when a tool produces an output. It should create space for learners to inspect, question and revise that output.
